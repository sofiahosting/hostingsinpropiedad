Deliverable 1 · Creator ops workflow

The pipeline I'd build, stage by stage.

Seven stages, each with an owner, a standard and a number. The goal: any client brief can go from "approved" to "delivered, on time, on brief" without the Co-Founder touching it.

01

Source

Owner: Creator sourcers

Process

  • Roster gaps reviewed weekly vs. the next 30 days of briefs (niche × demo × content type)
  • Channels: TikTok/IG search, TikTok Shop affiliates, marketplaces, referrals from top creators
  • Outreach templates by niche; every lead logged in the database the same day

Standard

  • No brief should ever start with "do we have someone for this?"
  • Always 2–3 backup creators per active niche

KPIs

  • Reply rate on outreach
  • Time-to-fill a roster gap
  • Bench depth per niche
02

Vet

Owner: Outreach coordinator → me

Process

  • Portfolio scored on a 1–5 rubric: hook, on-camera energy, lighting/audio, editing, brief-following
  • Paid test piece for anyone going into Tier A
  • Rate, usage terms and turnaround confirmed before first brief

Standard

  • Rubric ≥ 3.5 to join roster
  • No creator goes live with a client without one internal test

KPIs

  • Pass rate by source
  • First-brief success (approved with ≤1 revision)
03

Onboard

Owner: Outreach coordinator

Process

  • Welcome pack: how we brief, file naming, delivery specs, payment timeline, usage rights explained in plain English
  • "What great looks like" library: 5 winning ads per niche
  • Contract + payment details in one step

Standard

  • Onboarded in ≤ 48h from yes
  • Creator knows exactly when they'll be paid

KPIs

  • Time-to-first-brief
  • Onboarding drop-off
04

Brief & match

Owner: Me + creative strategist

Process

  • One-page brief standard: goal, audience, the ONE message, 3 hooks to test, must-say / never-say, specs, deadline
  • Match on past performance in the same niche first, then demo fit, then rate
  • Brief confirmed by creator in writing (questions answered before shooting, not after)

Standard

  • No brief leaves without hooks + do/don't list
  • Every asset gets a test-ready name

KPIs

  • Brief clarity (questions per brief)
  • Match hit rate vs. performance
05

Produce & QA

Owner: Me (QA) + creators

Process

  • Check-in at 50% of the timeline; red flag at 75% triggers a backup creator
  • QA checklist before anything reaches the client: hook ≤2s, audio, captions, framing, claims compliance, specs
  • Max 2 revision rounds, with specific notes, never "make it pop"

Standard

  • Client never sees a file that failed internal QA
  • Revisions: timestamped, actionable

KPIs

  • On-time rate (target 95%+)
  • First-pass approval
  • Avg. revision rounds
06

Deliver & measure

Owner: Me + media buyer

Process

  • Delivery folder standard: raws, hooks as separate cuts, captions file, usage terms
  • Asset naming ties each file to creator + hook + angle, so ad data maps back to the creator
  • Pull hook rate, hold rate, CTR, CPA per creator every 2 weeks

Standard

  • Every creator has a performance record, not just a quality opinion

KPIs

  • Hook rate / hold rate per creator
  • Win rate (assets that beat control)
07

Retain & tier

Owner: Me

Process

  • Monthly tiering (A / B / C) from the scorecard
  • Tier A: first pick on briefs, faster payment, feedback shared from the ad data
  • Payment on time, always — the #1 retention lever from the creator side

Standard

  • Top creators hear their results, not just "thanks"
  • C-tier gets one coaching round, then off-roster

KPIs

  • Repeat-creator %
  • Tier A churn
  • Avg. days to pay

Deliverable 3 · Short case study

Turning one creator into a performance-creative pipeline

Bonzo, a tax firm for Airbnb hosts, paid me as a creator for Meta Ads (Click-to-Messenger). The ads ran from their account; I owned concept, scripting and production.

UGCCreative testingNaming systemsMeta AdsBrief templates

The bottleneck

Paid social needs volume to test. But every video depended on me filming on camera — my calendar was the brand's creative ceiling, and ad fatigue was coming faster than new videos.

The system

  • A second format: "no-camera" scripts (B-roll + on-screen text + voiceover), so production didn't depend on on-camera time
  • A fixed script template: Hook (0–3s) / Visual / VO or on-screen text / CTA — the same structure every time, so a new angle takes minutes to brief
  • A naming convention: campaigns as META | Hosts [dest] | [month], ads as Bonzo mensajes Video [N] Sofia — every result maps back to one creative
  • Separate angles per video (surprise tax bill, before/after, etc.) so we test messages, not just edits

Why it matters for your agency

This is creator ops in miniature: remove the dependency, standardize the brief, name for data. At agency scale, the same three moves apply to 100 creators instead of one.

Second example, same thinking: for a live event with 225 attendees I wrote a run-of-show SOP (before / during / after, with every link and escalation rule) so a helper could run chat support, link drops and FAQ capture without me. One owner per step, a written trigger for every action, and a debrief list at the end. That's how I'd hand work to creator sourcers and coordinators.

Deliverable 4 · First 90 days

Listen, standardize, then scale.

I won't rebuild what already works. The first month is about finding the real bottleneck with data, not assumptions.

Days 1–30

Audit & baseline

  • Map the current pipeline brief → delivery; time every step
  • Clean the creator database; fill rates, availability, tier
  • Baseline KPIs: on-time %, revisions, first-pass approval
  • 1:1s with sourcers, coordinators and strategists
  • Interview 10 top creators: why they stay, what annoys them
Output: bottleneck report + KPI dashboard for the Co-Founder
Days 31–60

Standards & SOPs

  • Ship brief template, QA checklist and delivery spec
  • Launch the scorecard and first A/B/C tiering
  • SOPs for sourcing, vetting and onboarding, owned by the team
  • Backup-creator rule for at-risk deadlines
  • Close roster gaps in the top 3 niches by demand
Output: fewer revisions, fewer late files, measurable
Days 61–90

Scale & loop

  • Feed ad performance back into matching (winners by niche)
  • Tier A program: priority briefs, faster payment
  • Sourcing targets per sourcer, tied to upcoming briefs
  • Explore TikTok Shop affiliates as a sourcing channel
  • Monthly ops report: what moved, what's next
Output: a pipeline that runs without the Co-Founder in it
