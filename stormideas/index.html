Client relationships

For two years I was Marketing Director at Gersten Financial Planning in San Diego, the client-facing marketing lead. I handled up to 5 clients at once: briefs, timelines, QA, KPI reporting and client meetings.

I also ran 30+ short-term rentals, mostly owned by other people. Every owner got a monthly meeting: revenue, expenses and the plan for next month.

Same rule in both jobs: clients should know what happens next before they have to ask. That's how you build confidence, and confident clients are the ones whose accounts grow.

Creative judgment

From vision to brief

For my brand Daily Me I briefed 50+ creators: the hook, the key messages and exactly what to show on camera. Then I tracked which videos actually sold, and fed that back into the next brief.

A good brief makes the creative better and the review shorter. Same with feedback: if it can't be acted on, it isn't feedback.

04

Catching risk early

  1. Early test

    An ad was getting very cheap clicks.

  2. Diagnosed

    The hook promised more than the service could deliver.

  3. Flagged, with a fix

    I raised it before scaling the budget, not after, and proposed an honest rewrite.

  4. Rewritten

    An honest hook. Higher cost per click, better-fit leads.

How I'd work at Storm

  1. Step 01

    Listen & map stakeholders

    Strategy, stakeholders, commercial context and pressure points. Who decides, who approves, what success looks like.

    Brief with clear owners and dates

    Ambiguity turned into owners, dependencies and dates, in records people and agents can pick up.

    Review with specific feedback

    Protect the client's intent, improve the work, challenge what's off-strategy or wrong for the platform.

    Report, flag risks, find growth

    Scope pressure, risks and opportunities surfaced early, with evidence, options and a recommendation.
